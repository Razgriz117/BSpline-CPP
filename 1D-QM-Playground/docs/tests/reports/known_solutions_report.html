What the solver actually solves

Before comparing to any textbook, it helps to see the problem the code poses, because three of its conventions decide what "the known solution" even is.

equation
\(-\tfrac12\,\phi'' + V(x)\,\phi = E\,\phi\) on the finite interval domain, with mass 1 hard-coded.
boundaries
The first and last B-splines are dropped, so every bound eigenstate satisfies \(\phi(x_{\min}) = \phi(x_{\max}) = 0\): hard walls at both ends. On \([0,100]\) this makes every "1D" problem a radial problem — the wall at \(x=0\) is the origin, and only solutions vanishing there survive.
box states
Because the box is finite, the positive-energy spectrum is discrete too. The eigenstate_NNN.png files continue past the true bound states into these box states; they are artefacts of the walls, not of the potential.
plots
eigenstate_N.png shows the density \(|\phi_n|^2\) (so it is never negative and has twice as many bumps as you might expect). continuum_N.png shows \(\psi_E\) itself.
continuum
Energy-normalised: far from the potential \(\psi_E(x)\to\sqrt{2/\pi k}\,\sin(kx+\delta)\), \(k=\sqrt{2E}\). The phase shift written to phase_shifts.dat is \(\delta = \arctan\!\big(k\psi/\psi'\big)\big|_{x=R} - kR\) with the principal-branch arctangent, so it is only meaningful modulo \(\pi\), and it comes out as a large negative number (e.g. \(-14\pi\)) that must be wrapped before reading.

1 · Free particle on [0, 100]

Input. V = 0 on \([0,100]\), 51 nodes (spacing \(h=2\)), order 12, continuum at \(E = 0.1, 0.2, \dots, 0.5\).

The known solution

A free particle with hard walls is the particle in a box — the one problem everyone solves first, which is exactly why it is the right first test.

1

With \(V=0\) the equation is \(\phi'' = -2E\,\phi\). Writing \(k^2 = 2E\) the solutions are \(\sin kx\) and \(\cos kx\).

2

\(\phi(0)=0\) kills the cosine. \(\phi(L)=0\) then forces \(\sin kL = 0\), i.e. \(k_n = n\pi/L\). Picture the wave being pinned at both ends like a guitar string: only whole numbers of half-wavelengths fit.

3

Energies and normalised states follow immediately: \[E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{2L^2} = n^2 \times 4.9348\ldots\times10^{-4}, \qquad \phi_n(x)=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{L}}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}.\] The density peaks at \(2/L = 0.02\) — the height of every bump in the solver's eigenstate_N.png.

4

A continuum state at energy \(E\) is simply \(\sqrt{2/\pi k}\,\sin kx\). Nothing scatters it, so the phase shift is \(\delta = 0\) and \(d\delta/dE = 0\).

2 · Finite square well

Input. \(V=-1\) on \([0,10)\), \(V=0\) on \([10,100]\); 51 nodes (\(h=2\)), order 12, continuum at \(E=0.1\ldots0.5\).

The known solution

The wall at \(x=0\) changes the problem you should look up. This is not the symmetric well of the textbooks on \([-a,a]\) — it is its right half with \(\phi(0)=0\). The odd states of the symmetric well vanish at the origin, so this half-well has exactly the odd solutions of a symmetric well of half-width \(a=10\) and depth \(V_0=1\). (It is also the \(\ell=0\) radial problem for a spherical well, which is why it looks like a nucleus.)

1

Inside (\(x \lt a\)) the particle has kinetic energy \(E+V_0>0\), so it oscillates: \(\phi = A\sin Kx\) with \(K=\sqrt{2(E+V_0)}\). The sine, not the cosine, because \(\phi(0)=0\).

2

Outside (\(x>a\)) a bound state has \(E<0\) and must decay: \(\phi = B e^{-\kappa x}\), \(\kappa=\sqrt{-2E}\). Picture the wave sloshing inside the well and leaking a decaying tail through the wall.

3

Glue the two pieces at \(x=a\) so that \(\phi\) and \(\phi'\) are continuous. Dividing the two conditions removes \(A\) and \(B\) and leaves one equation in \(E\): \[K\cot(Ka) = -\kappa .\] Both sides are functions of \(E\); a bound state is a crossing. \(\cot\) runs from \(+\infty\) down to \(-\infty\) on every interval \(((m-\tfrac12)\pi, m\pi)\) of \(Ka\), while \(-\kappa\) is a smooth negative curve, so there is one crossing per interval where \(Ka\) can reach \((m-\tfrac12)\pi\) before \(E\) hits zero.

4

How many? At \(E\to0^-\), \(Ka\to\sqrt{2V_0}\,a = \sqrt{200} = 14.1421\). Compare to \(4.5\pi = 14.1372\). The fifth state's threshold is exceeded by \(0.005\) — so the infinite-domain well has five bound states, the fifth one bound by only \(E_5 = -2.3\times10^{-5}\), with a decay length \(1/\kappa = 147\) bohr.

5

Solving \(K\cot Ka=-\kappa\) numerically (Brent's method on each bracket) gives \(E = -0.956996,\;-0.828523,\;-0.616572,\;-0.326732,\;-0.000023\).

6

But the solver's domain ends at \(x=100\) with a wall. A tail with decay length 147 does not fit in a 90-bohr corridor. Replacing \(e^{-\kappa x}\) by the hard-wall solution \(\sinh\kappa(100-x)\) changes the matching to \(K\cot Ka = -\kappa\coth\kappa(100-a)\), and that equation has no root in the fifth bracket: the wall squeezes the fifth state up into the positive-energy box spectrum. So the correct answer for this input is four bound states, then box states starting a little above \(\pi^2/(2\cdot100^2)=4.9\times10^{-4}\).

What the solver produced

Four negative eigenvalues — structurally exactly right, including the delicate fifth-state story. The numbers, however, are only good to three or four digits, and the first positive-energy box states are worse: the shipped grid puts them at \(5.6\times10^{-4}, 2.2\times10^{-3}, 5.0\times10^{-3}\), above the empty-box ladder \(4.9\times10^{-4}, 2.0\times10^{-3}, 4.4\times10^{-3}\), whereas solving \(100k+\delta(E)=j\pi\) exactly places them at \(5.5\times10^{-5}, 1.16\times10^{-3}, 3.37\times10^{-3}\), below it, as an attractive well demands — the lowest one being the ghost of the fifth bound state. At 401 nodes the solver gives \(5.9\times10^{-5}, 1.17\times10^{-3}, 3.38\times10^{-3}\).

Every solver energy lies above the exact one (a variational method can never undershoot), and each halving of the node spacing cuts the error by a factor that grows from 4 toward 8: convergence like \(h^3\), far slower than the \(10^{-13}\) the free particle achieved on the same grid. The reason is the step in \(V\) at \(x=10\). The exact \(\phi\) has a kink in its second derivative there, but an order-12 B-spline basis on simple knots is \(C^{10}\)-smooth across every node, so it can only approximate that kink by bending over the neighbouring intervals. (The cure the B-spline notes describe — repeating the knot at \(x=10\) so the basis loses smoothness exactly there — is implemented in tise.cpp as strategic node placement but is not reachable from tise_solver, which always builds a uniform grid.)

Continuum and phase shifts

For \(E>0\) both regions oscillate: \(\sin Kx\) inside, \(\sin(kx+\delta)\) outside. Matching the logarithmic derivative at \(x=a\), \(K\cot Ka = k\cot(ka+\delta)\), gives the classic result \[\delta(E) = \arctan\!\Big(\frac{k}{K}\tan Ka\Big) - ka \pmod\pi .\] Two things to see in the picture: the inside wave has shorter wavelength (higher local momentum) and smaller amplitude (the current \(\propto K|A|^2\) must match \(k|B|^2\)), and the outside wave is shifted forward relative to a free one — that shift is \(\delta\).

On the 401-node grid they become \(1.4641, -0.1307, -1.2534, 1.2024, 1.0675\) — all five within \(5\times10^{-4}\) of the exact column. So the continuum machinery is correct; the 51-node grid is not fine enough. Inside the well at \(E=0.5\) the local energy is \(E+V_0=1.5\), the wavelength is \(3.6\) bohr, and \(h=2\) puts only 1.8 nodes per wavelength — below even the Nyquist limit. The solver's own \(E_{\rm acc}=\pi^2/2h^2=1.23\) check did not fire because it compares \(E_{\max}\) to the node spacing without subtracting \(V_{\min}\).

The \(d\delta/dE\) column is not usable at this energy grid, for a reason independent of resolution: it is a central difference of \(\sin2\delta\) over \(\Delta E=0.1\), but \(\delta\) changes by one to two radians between neighbouring energies, so the difference quotient is sampling a function it cannot see. The exact derivative is large and negative (\(-19\) at \(E=0.1\)), decreasing toward zero: the same slope you would read off \(\delta(E)\) in any square-well scattering figure.

Verdict — physically correct, numerically under-resolved. Four bound states with the right node structure and the right treatment of the marginal fifth state; energies converge to the exact values as \(h^3\) and are within \(2\times10^{-4}\)–\(6\times10^{-3}\) on the shipped grid. The near-threshold box states are qualitatively wrong on the shipped grid (lowest one off by 10×) and also converge on refinement. Phase shifts converge too. To make this a clean pass, either raise n_nodes to 201–401 or expose the repeated-knot option at the potential step; and treat dδ/dE as not yet computed.

3 · Harmonic oscillator

Input. \(V=\tfrac12x^2\) on \([-20,20]\), 81 nodes (\(h=0.5\)), order 12, no continuum.

The known solution

1

In these units \(\omega=1\), so the equation is \(-\tfrac12\phi''+\tfrac12x^2\phi=E\phi\). Far out, \(x^2\) dominates and \(\phi\sim e^{-x^2/2}\); a Gaussian is the only way the wave can be squeezed by a parabola on both sides.

2

Write \(\phi = H(x)e^{-x^2/2}\). The series for \(H\) must terminate or \(\phi\) blows up like \(e^{+x^2/2}\); it terminates only when \(E = n+\tfrac12\). The polynomials are the Hermite polynomials \(H_n\).

3

Normalised states: \[\phi_n(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!\,\sqrt\pi}}\,H_n(x)\,e^{-x^2/2},\qquad E_n=n+\tfrac12 .\] The ground-state density peaks at \(1/\sqrt\pi = 0.5642\); the solver's eigenstate_1.png peaks at 0.564.

4

The box at \(\pm20\) is irrelevant for any state whose classical turning point \(\sqrt{2E}\) is well inside it — that is every state up to \(n\approx200\). The real limit here is the node spacing, not the walls.

A useful rule from these numbers: to get \(10^{-6}\) in the energy, keep at least about 3.3 nodes per local wavelength, \(h \lesssim 2\pi/(3.3\sqrt{2(E-V_{\min})})\).

4 · Hydrogen, ℓ = 1

Input. \(V = -1/x + 1/x^2\) on \((0,100]\), 51 nodes (\(h=2\)), order 12, continuum at \(E=0.1\ldots0.5\).

The known solution

1

Separating the 3D Coulomb problem in spherical coordinates and writing \(\psi = \frac{u(r)}{r}Y_{\ell m}\) leaves a 1D equation for \(u\): \[-\tfrac12 u'' + \Big(-\frac1r + \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2r^2}\Big)u = Eu,\qquad u(0)=0 .\] The \(1/r^2\) term is the centrifugal barrier — the price of angular momentum. The YAML's \(+1/x^2\) is \(\ell(\ell+1)/2 = 1\), i.e. \(\ell=1\): this test is the p-wave radial problem, not the s-wave one. The wall at \(x=0\) is precisely the \(u(0)=0\) condition.

2

The bound spectrum is \(E_n=-1/2n^2\) with \(n\ge\ell+1\), so for \(\ell=1\) the lowest state is 2p: \[E = -0.125,\ -0.055\overline5,\ -0.03125,\ -0.02,\ -0.0138\overline8,\ -0.01020,\ \ldots\] There is no \(-0.5\) in this test and there should not be.

3

The radial functions are \(u_{n1}(r) = r\,R_{n1}(r)\) with \(R_{n\ell}\propto e^{-r/n}(2r/n)^\ell L^{2\ell+1}_{n-\ell-1}(2r/n)\). Each \(u_{n1}\) has \(n-2\) radial nodes, and its density peaks near \(r\approx n^2\): 4 bohr for 2p, 12 for 3p, 25 for 4p — read those off the solver's plots.

4

An orbit of size \(n^2\) with an exponential tail \(e^{-r/n}\) needs a box several times \(n^2\). With \(R=100\), states up to 5p (mean radius 37) fit; 6p (mean radius 53, tail reaching past 100) and beyond are squeezed and their energies rise.

5

For \(E>0\) the regular solution is the Coulomb function \(F_\ell(\eta,kr)\) with Sommerfeld parameter \(\eta=-1/k\). Its asymptotic form \(\sin(kr - \ell\pi/2 - \eta\ln 2kr + \sigma_\ell)\) never settles to a fixed phase because of the logarithm — a Coulomb tail is not "flat" at any finite \(R\).

Summary and what to change

Nothing in the four outputs contradicts the known physics. Every discrepancy found traces to one of three causes that are visible in the input files, not to the solver's equations.

a

Node spacing. \(h=2\) on the free-particle and well tests, \(h=0.5\) on the oscillator, is enough for the low states only. The empirical threshold from all four runs is about 3.3 nodes per local wavelength for \(10^{-6}\) accuracy; the code's \(E_{\rm acc}=\pi^2/2h^2\) warning corresponds to 2 nodes per wavelength and ignores the potential depth, so it is silent for the well's \(E=0.5\) case even though that case is under-resolved by any measure. Refining the well to 201 nodes and the oscillator to 161 nodes turned every marginal number above into a clean pass.

b

Smoothness across the potential step. The well converges only as \(h^3\) because the basis is \(C^{10}\) at \(x=10\). A repeated knot there (already in tise.cpp, not exposed to tise_solver) would restore spectral convergence.

c

\(d\delta/dE\). A five-point energy grid with \(\Delta E=0.1\) cannot differentiate a phase that moves by radians per step; the current column is noise for every potential except the free particle. Either take a fine energy grid around each point of interest or differentiate the matching formula analytically.

If your WSL images differ from the ones described here — a different number of bound states, densities peaking at other heights, continuum amplitudes other than \(\sqrt{2/\pi k}\) — the most likely cause is a different commit or an edited YAML, and I would want to see the PNGs directly.
